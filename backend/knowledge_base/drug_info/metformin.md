# Metformin (Glucophage)

## Drug Class
Biguanide — oral diabetes medication

## Common Uses
- Type 2 diabetes management
- Prediabetes prevention
- Polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS)

## Standard Adult Dosage
Start: 500mg once or twice daily with meals.
Maintenance: 1500mg to 2000mg per day in divided doses.
Maximum: 2550mg per day.
Always take with food to reduce stomach upset.

## How It Works
Metformin reduces glucose production in the liver and improves
insulin sensitivity. It does not cause hypoglycemia when used alone.

## Key Drug Interactions
- Contrast dye (iodinated): stop metformin 48 hours before and after
  imaging procedures using contrast dye — risk of kidney damage.
- Alcohol: increases risk of lactic acidosis.
- Diuretics: may affect kidney function and metformin levels.

## Who Should Avoid Metformin
- Patients with severe kidney disease (eGFR below 30)
- Patients with liver disease
- Patients with heart failure
- Before surgery requiring general anesthesia

## Side Effects
- Common: nausea, diarrhea, stomach upset (usually improves after 2 weeks)
- Rare but serious: lactic acidosis (muscle pain, weakness, difficulty
  breathing) — seek emergency care immediately

## Monitoring Required
Regular blood tests for kidney function (creatinine) and HbA1c every
3 to 6 months to monitor diabetes control.
