# Diabetes — Patient Guide

## Types of Diabetes

### Type 1 Diabetes
- Immune system destroys insulin-producing cells
- Requires insulin injections for life
- Usually diagnosed in children and young adults

### Type 2 Diabetes
- Body does not use insulin effectively
- Most common type — linked to lifestyle and genetics
- Managed with diet, exercise, and medications

### Gestational Diabetes
- Develops during pregnancy
- Usually resolves after delivery but increases future risk

## Warning Signs of Diabetes
- Frequent urination, especially at night
- Excessive thirst
- Unexplained weight loss
- Blurred vision
- Slow healing of cuts and wounds
- Frequent infections
- Tingling or numbness in hands and feet

## Blood Sugar Targets
- Fasting blood sugar: 4.0 to 7.0 mmol/L
- 2 hours after meal: below 10.0 mmol/L
- HbA1c: below 7% (checked every 3 to 6 months)

## Hypoglycemia (Low Blood Sugar) — Below 4.0 mmol/L
Symptoms: sweating, shaking, confusion, rapid heartbeat.
Treatment: take 15g fast-acting carbohydrate immediately:
- 3 to 4 glucose tablets, OR
- 150ml fruit juice, OR
- 3 teaspoons of sugar in water
Recheck blood sugar after 15 minutes. If still low, repeat.
If unconscious — call 1990 immediately, do not give food or drink.

## Hyperglycemia (High Blood Sugar) — Above 15 mmol/L
Symptoms: extreme thirst, frequent urination, fatigue, blurred vision.
Contact your doctor or clinic if blood sugar stays high.
Go to emergency if vomiting, difficulty breathing, or confusion.

## Foot Care for Diabetic Patients
Diabetes reduces blood flow and sensation in feet.
- Check feet daily for cuts, blisters, or sores
- Wash feet daily in warm water, dry carefully between toes
- Never walk barefoot
- Wear well-fitting shoes and clean cotton socks
- Report any foot wounds to your doctor immediately
